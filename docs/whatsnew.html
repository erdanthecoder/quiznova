Quoldek 3.0

Levels, coins and monsters you have to earn. Eleven games instead of eight, a settings screen for every live round, a different piece of music for every part of a game, Laser Tag rebuilt in real 3D, and an app you can install on Windows.

Arrives today at 12:00 — Everything below goes live at midday.

Levels and coins Three new games Game settings Laser Tag in 3D Music The Windows app Writing quizzes

Three new games

Every game we had was a race in the same shape: answer, score, watch your thing get bigger. These three are not that. Same quiz, same PIN — pick one on the way in.

Eleven games now. Normal, Laser Tag, Kart Race, Tower Build, Treasure Run, Boss Battle, Snowball Fight, Balloon Drop, and the three above. Each has three maps, and everybody picks their own monster.

Levels, coins, and monsters you earn

Playing now counts for something that lasts past the final whistle. Every game pays coins, coins buy monster parts, and a level opens the better ones.

Coins for playing, not just for winning

Answering pays. Answering correctly pays more. A run of right answers pays more again, and the placings at the end are a flourish on top. Coming last still earns something — otherwise the same three children own everything by half term and nobody else bothers.

Twenty levels

Your level comes from every coin you have ever earned and is never spent, so it only goes up. The first levels arrive quickly — one game should show you something happening — and after that each costs a little more than the last.

Monsters that have to be earned

Colours, shapes, eyes, mouths and patterns come in five tiers, from Starter to Legendary. A locked one tells you what it costs, or what level opens it — never just a padlock, because "no" without a reason is the most annoying thing a game can say. The plainest ones are free from the very first game, so nobody stares at an empty monster maker.

No account, and nothing about a child leaves the phone

Children join with a name and a PIN — there is nothing to sign up for and nothing to remember. So the coins and the level are kept on the phone that earned them, and the screen says so plainly rather than pretending they follow anybody around.

The results screen after every game shows exactly what each part of playing was worth, so the number is not just handed over — a total nobody can account for is a total nobody trusts.

You decide how the round plays

Hosting used to ask two questions and start. There is a proper settings screen now, and none of it touches the quiz — so the same quiz can be a fast five minutes for one class and a slow careful round for another.

Seconds a question

Leave it as the quiz says, or set 10 to 60 for this game only. Every screen counts down the same number.

Points a question, and a double last question

Change what a question is worth, and let the final one be worth double so a class behind still has a way back.

How it ends

All the questions, a race to 500 or 1,000, or a clock — 5, 10 or 15 minutes, and whoever is ahead wins.

Streaks, shuffling and late joiners

Turn the streak bonus off, shuffle the questions or the answers, and decide whether somebody arriving late can still join.

Laser Tag, rebuilt in real 3D

It was a floor plan: a flat top-down diagram where a player at the far end was the same size as one at your feet. There is a camera now, standing above and behind and tilted at the floor, so the arena has depth and a horizon.

Everything stands up

Figures have height and cast a shadow onto the floor, so they are on the ground rather than in front of it. Beams travel at chest height. Somebody at the far wall is genuinely smaller than somebody beside you.

A room, not a diagram

The floor is a plane with a grid that converges, walls all the way round lit along the top, and the arena runs to a horizon. The board holds all of it; a phone rides behind its own player.

No download, no plugin

It is arithmetic on the same canvas that was already there — no 3D library and nothing to fetch — so it still runs on a school laptop and with the wifi unplugged.

Music that follows the game

There was one tune, playing from the moment the board opened until the game ended. A room does not experience a game that way.

A different piece for each part of a round

Something unhurried while everybody joins. Something with a pulse when a question is open — and as the clock runs down it speeds up, leans on every beat, and climbs. A breath while the answer is up. Something bright to read the standings to.

Played, not downloaded

Every note is built by the browser out of a few oscillators, so it adds nothing to load and sounds the same offline. It only ever plays on the big screen — thirty phones each playing their own copy would be unbearable.

Quoldek for Windows

Not a browser pointed at the website. It runs the whole thing on your own computer — the pages, the quizzes and the live game — and phones join over your own wifi.

Works with no internet at all

School wifi is unreliable and school firewalls block things, and neither can stop a lesson that never leaves the building. Your quizzes are files in a folder you can back up, the board goes on the projector, and Join a game lets you play along on the same computer — or on somebody else's, over the wifi.

Writing the quiz

The co-pilot shows what it knows

Thirty-eight subjects it can write questions about — easy maths, times tables, spelling, synonyms, the human body, the water cycle, dinosaurs — listed on screen with a year group beside them. Tap one and the questions are written. No guessing what the tool knows.

Paste anything in

Questions from Gemini or ChatGPT, a numbered list, a table, a block of prose with the answers starred — it reads all of it, and tells you what it found before anything is added.

Fixes people actually asked for

Joining a game shows the room straight away instead of an empty lobby. Closing a tab, locking a phone or losing the wifi for a moment no longer looks like the game ending — it waits and reconnects. Both are live now.

How much Quoldek is really played

The front page used to say "24 playing" whatever was happening — a decoration pretending to be a fact. It now shows only what the database can prove: games actually hosted and people who actually joined one, counted by the database itself so no page can inflate it. When there is nothing true to say, it says nothing.
